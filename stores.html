Spares list

Every item in the stores, one by one: spares, consumables (diesel, oils, filters, explosives), insurance spares (stand-by units for critical machines, kept even if unused) and repairable units (sent for repair, not bought new). Reorder level = daily use × (lead time + safety days): when stock plus what is already on order falls to this level, raise the indent. Dead stock = not issued for over a year. Overstock = more than reorder level plus 12 months of use.

Draft indent

Every item at or below its reorder level, missing insurance spares, units to send for repair, and fitted spares whose indent is due within 30 days. Each line has a quantity, value, purchase route and a one-line justification.

ABC analysis

Spares ranked by yearly spend (price × use per month × 12). A few costly spares take most of the money (A); many others take little (C). Watch A items closely; keep C items simple. The grid below adds how critical each spare is, because a spare with little spend can still stop a machine.

Indent early warning

Spares fitted on machines wear out. Each card works out when the part will wear out, from the hours it has run and how many hours a day its machine works. It then counts back the lead time and a safety margin to give the date to raise the indent.

Import from SAP

Bring in lists exported from SAP (ECC or S/4HANA) instead of typing them. In the SAP report choose List → Export → Spreadsheet and save it from Excel as CSV, or choose List → Save → File → Text with tabs. Your file stays on your computer; only the rows you import go into the tool.
